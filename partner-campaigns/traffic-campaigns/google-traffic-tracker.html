Measurement Methodology & Data Source Comparison

This dashboard pulls from three data sources that measure overlapping but distinct things. Understanding the differences is critical for accurate reporting — especially when comparing traffic sources like Google Demand Gen, GDN, and third-party vendors (e.g., JunGroup).

Google Ads
Measures: Clicks, impressions, spend, CPC, CTR
Scope: Ad platform only — counts the click event, not what happens after
Use for: Media efficiency, budget pacing, audience segment comparison
Parse.ly
Measures: Pageviews, engaged minutes, avg. time on article
Method: Heartbeat pixel (checks every ~5s if user is actively scrolling/clicking)
Scope: Article-level only — measures attention on that specific page
Use for: Content quality assessment, real reader engagement, traffic source quality comparison
Google Analytics 4
Measures: Sessions, views/session, avg. session duration
Method: Event-based — calculates time between first and last event in a session
Scope: Full session (includes navigation beyond landing article)
Use for: Site-wide engagement, ad impression opportunity (more time = more slots served)
Why Numbers Don't Match Across Sources
Parse.ly Engagement > GA4 Engagement (common with DG/GDN)
Parse.ly actively tracks attention via heartbeat; GA4 relies on event gaps. For single-page sessions (user reads article, then leaves), GA4 often records 0s because there's no second event to measure against. Parse.ly still captures the 20s of active reading. Parse.ly is more accurate for article-level engagement.
GA4 Session Duration > Parse.ly Article Time (expected)
GA4 measures the full session — if a user lands on an article and then clicks to another FOS page, that additional browse time is captured in GA4 but not in Parse.ly's article-scoped metric. The gap between the two = additional site exploration. This is a good signal for ad impression opportunity.
JunGroup / Incentivized Traffic: GA4 Data is Unreliable
JunGroup is a reward-based content distribution network — users view content in exchange for in-app rewards (game currency, premium access, etc.). This creates a specific data artifact:

Parse.ly shows very low engagement (accurate): The heartbeat pixel correctly detects that users aren't actively scrolling or reading — they opened the page to trigger the reward and moved on.

GA4 shows inflated 5+ minute sessions (misleading): GA4 calculates session duration as time between first and last event. JunGroup traffic commonly opens pages in background tabs that persist for minutes while users do other things. If any secondary event fires (scroll trigger, visibility API, ad refresh), GA4 records all elapsed time as "session duration" — even though no one was reading.

Bottom line: For comparing traffic source quality, Parse.ly engagement is the trustworthy metric. GA4 session duration should not be used to evaluate incentivized traffic sources. When assessing JunGroup vs. Google DG/GDN, compare Parse.ly avg. minutes — not GA4 session duration.

Key Learnings — Invesco & Capital One (For Future Campaigns)
Findings that should inform Fidelity × F1 and all future Demand Gen flights

1. Article Concentration Risk Is Real

Invesco's top 3 articles consumed 76% of total clicks and 72% of spend — the algorithm heavily favored a small number of high-CTR articles. Capital One's distribution was healthier: the top 3 accounted for only 46% of clicks across 20 articles.

For Fidelity/F1: Seed with 8–10+ articles from the start. Monitor for single-article dominance in the first 48 hours and be ready to pause top performers if the algorithm over-indexes.

2. Desktop Campaigns Drive Better Session Quality

Desktop DG consistently drove longer sessions: Invesco desktop 41.5s vs. mobile 22.5s (1.8x). GDN desktop was even stronger: 53.6s (Invesco) and 90.0s (Capital One).

Desktop traffic also grew to 14.5% of all Parse.ly PVs, with desktop + tablet at 22.9%. More time on site = more ad impressions served per visit.

For Fidelity/F1: Launch desktop DG from day one alongside mobile. Budget split recommendation: 40% mobile DG / 40% desktop DG / 20% GDN.

3. Maximize Clicks Bidding Works — Don't Overthink It

All campaigns used Maximize Clicks, and CPCs came in well below expectations: $0.112 (Invesco), $0.133 (Capital One), vs. Toyota's $0.059 benchmark. The algorithm efficiently optimized across audience segments without manual bid intervention.

For Fidelity/F1: Launch with Maximize Clicks on all campaign types. Set daily budget caps aligned to total budget ÷ flight days.

4. Narrative Content Drives 3–5x More Engagement

Feature-length, narrative articles consistently outperformed quick news hits on Parse.ly engagement: Tiger DUI (23.4s), Veteran Coaches (16.2s), Hubert Davis (17.3s) vs. news-driven articles averaging 3–6s. This directly maps to ad impressions served per visit.

For Fidelity/F1: Prioritize in-depth F1 feature stories over race results or quick news hits. Editorial should front-load 2–3 narrative pieces before campaign launch.

5. CTR and Engagement Don't Always Correlate

Invesco's highest-CTR article (NC/Hubert Davis, 7.03%) had strong engagement (17.3s), but the second-highest (Mid-Majors, 5.29%) was even better (17.5s). Meanwhile, Capital One's top-CTR article (Tiger/TGL, 7.11%) had excellent engagement (17.5s), while Denver Summit (0.52% CTR) had low engagement (6.2s). High CTR alone doesn't guarantee quality attention.

For Fidelity/F1: Track both CTR and Parse.ly engagement from day one. Consider pausing articles with high CTR but sub-5s Parse.ly engagement — they're driving clicks but not delivering ad value.

6. GDN Is Expensive but Drives Deepest Sessions

GDN CPC was significantly higher: $0.334 (Invesco) and $0.418 (Capital One) vs. ~$0.11 for DG. But GDN desktop sessions averaged 53.6s (Invesco) and 90.0s (Capital One) — far exceeding even the untargeted benchmark (68.1s). The trade-off: fewer clicks, but each click generates substantially more ad inventory.

For Fidelity/F1: Allocate 15–20% of budget to GDN for session depth. Consider GDN as the "quality layer" on top of DG's volume engine.

7. Audience Segments Performed Roughly Equal

Across both campaigns, audience segments delivered similar CPCs: Invesco's Lookalike ($0.114) ≈ Sports Business ($0.111) ≈ March Madness ($0.114). Capital One's Google Signals ($0.116) ≈ Behavioral ($0.116). The algorithm equalized efficiency across all segments.

For Fidelity/F1: Layer 2–3 audience segments (e.g., crypto/finance interest via Google Signals + sports biz readers + F1 contextual) and let the algorithm optimize across them. No need to pick winners in advance.

8. Flight Length Matters — Longer Flights Scale Better

Toyota (18 days, $7K) delivered 118,635 clicks at $0.059 CPC. Invesco (14 days, $8.3K) delivered 74,282 clicks at $0.112 CPC. Capital One (13 days, $3.6K) delivered 27,389 clicks at $0.133 CPC. Shorter flights with targeted audiences cost more per click — the algorithm needs time to learn and optimize, and shorter flights compress learning into a higher-CPC window.

For Fidelity/F1: Push for a minimum 3-week flight. If budget allows, 4+ weeks gives the algorithm more runway to optimize, and late-flight CPCs tend to be lower than launch-week CPCs.

Cross-Functional Implications

Editorial: Front-load 2–3 narrative feature articles before campaign launch. Quick news hits drive CTR but not engagement. Coordinate content calendar with campaign flight dates.

Social: Organic amplification of campaign articles can compound paid traffic. Consider coordinated social pushes on launch day to signal quality to the algorithm.

Why GA4 Engagement Is Misleading for JunGroup
GA4 (Mar 1–31) vs. Parse.ly · Same articles, same traffic, wildly different engagement readings

The 7–15x Discrepancy

GA4 reports 37–183 seconds of engagement per active user on JunGroup articles. Parse.ly reports 4–12 seconds. That's a 7–15x gap — and it's not a measurement error. It's the signature of incentivized background-tab traffic.

What's happening: JunGroup users open the article to earn a reward, then the tab stays open in the background while they continue other tasks. GA4 counts the entire session as "engaged" because the page is still loaded. Parse.ly's heartbeat pixel detects that nobody is actually reading — and stops counting after a few seconds. Parse.ly is the trustworthy metric here.

Mario Ho: The Worst Offender

GA4 shows 182.9 seconds of engagement per user and 19.3 views per user — meaning each user loaded the article 19 times and GA4 counted 3 minutes of "engagement." Parse.ly measured 12.2 seconds of actual reading time. The 15x gap, combined with 9.6 views per session, is a textbook automated refresh loop.

What This Means for Reporting

Do not use GA4 engagement metrics for JunGroup traffic. The inflated session durations will make incentivized traffic look comparable to — or better than — organic and Google-driven traffic, which is not the case.

Parse.ly's heartbeat-based engagement is the only reliable measure for comparing traffic source quality. GA4 session duration is useful for Google DG/GDN campaigns (where sessions are real), but misleading for JunGroup.

The Impression Illusion

JunGroup generates 3.5M total impressions — but at 1.4 seconds per pageview, users aren't scrolling past the first ad slot. The majority of impressions fire on-page but likely never enter the viewport. GAM 360 viewability data would confirm the actual rate.

Google scenarios deliver far fewer total impressions, but users are spending 13–16 seconds per page and scrolling through 3–5 ad slots. The quality per impression is significantly higher.

What This Means for Advertisers

When ads are roadblocked on JunGroup articles, advertisers are paying for impressions that load on-page but may never actually be seen — the user is in a mobile game, the article is in a background webview, and scroll depth is minimal.

Google traffic delivers users who land on the article, scroll through it, and encounter ads in-view. The cost per viewable impression is likely comparable or better with Google — and the audience is real sports business readers, not gamers earning rewards.

Bottom Line

JunGroup delivers volume, not value. At 1.4 seconds of engagement per pageview and only ~1.4 ad impressions per visit, the traffic generates high impression counts on paper — but the majority are unlikely to be viewable. Abnormal return-visitor patterns further indicate the traffic is incentivized users loading pages in background tabs to earn rewards, not readers engaging with FOS content.

Reallocating $36.8K to Google in a single month would deliver 163K–271K sessions of real, engaged traffic depending on mix — with 12.9–14.8 seconds of Parse.ly engagement per pageview, 3–5 estimated ads seen per visit, and significantly higher viewability rates that translate to real advertiser value.

Recommendation: Shift JunGroup budget to Google Demand Gen. Use Scenario 2 for scale campaigns where volume and total impressions matter, Scenario 3 for premium advertiser campaigns where quality and viewability are the priority, or Scenario 1 as the balanced default.
