Why Opens Are Dropping: The Fluent Treadmill + Gmail Problem

The Core Diagnosis

The Memo's open decline is driven by two compounding forces: (1) Turning off Fluent at the end of 2025 removed a pipeline of "rented opens" from short-tenure subscribers, and (2) Gmail deliverability is suppressing opens on 66.7% of the subscriber base. Neither is about content quality — The Memo's 61.1% open rate is best-in-class among competitors.

The Fluent Treadmill Effect

Fluent was FOS's primary acquisition channel through 2025, adding 25K–36K subscribers per month. ~70% opened emails initially, creating a rolling pool of temporary openers.

Timeline: How the Treadmill Stopped

Sep–Nov 2025: Peak Fluent acquisition. 25K–36K new subs/month. 70% opening = 17K–25K fresh openers per cohort entering pipeline. Total opens sustained.
End of Dec 2025: Fluent turned off. Decision was correct — subscribers were unsubscribing quickly or never becoming active. But the transition cost is real.
Jan 2026: Last Fluent cohorts (Nov/Dec joiners) still in active window. Too soon to see impact.
Feb 2026: Oct/Nov cohorts aging out. Opens dip, but February is historically the lowest month — masked the signal.
Mar 2026: All Fluent cohorts past ~90-day active window. Opener pool hits zero. No new intake to replace them. Opens drop to 435K avg.

The Fluent → Gmail Feedback Loop

Fluent likely caused much of the Gmail problem. Months of adding high volumes of Fluent subscribers who never engaged taught Gmail's ML algorithm that Memo emails are low-priority. This tanked sender reputation, pushing more emails to the Promotions tab, which lowered opens further. Even with Fluent off, the reputation damage persists until engagement metrics consistently improve. The good news: purging inactive Fluent subscribers is already cleaning this up — organic churn has dropped 56% since September.

The Gmail Fix Math

66.7% of ~710K subscribers = ~473,570 on Gmail. At current 53.3% open rate: ~252,400 Gmail opens. If Gmail improves to 77.3% (Yahoo level): ~366,070 opens. That's +113,670 opens per send — nearly the entire gap to 550K. Gmail deliverability is the single highest-leverage lever available.

Strategic Recommendations

1Evaluate the Fluent Relationship

Fluent is responsible for 80.9% of all churn. The median Fluent subscriber stays 106 days before leaving. At the volumes FOS is acquiring through Fluent (32K–36K subscribers per month in Q4 2025), you're essentially filling a bucket that's leaking from the bottom. Calculate the true cost-per-active-subscriber for Fluent: if you're paying for subscribers who churn in 49 days, the effective cost is dramatically higher than the nominal CPL. Compare this to Meta Ads (BL) where median tenure is 362 days — 3.4x longer retention.

2Build an Onboarding Welcome Sequence

12% of Fluent unsubs leave within 3 days and 32.3% within 30 days. The first-touch experience is failing for a third of paid subscribers. Priority: Implement a 3-email welcome series that immediately demonstrates The Memo's value, establishes expectations on frequency/content, and drives a habit loop. Axios Sports' personal welcome email from the editor drove a 6% increase in open rates — replicate this.

3Address the Gmail Problem as an Unsub Driver

74.3% of user-initiated unsubscribes are Gmail users. Combined with the 53.3% Gmail open rate (vs. 77–85% on other domains), many Gmail subscribers may be unsubscribing because they're finding The Memo in their spam/promotions folder days late — not because they dislike the content. Priority: Include "move to Primary" instructions in the welcome sequence, and investigate Gmail authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and sender reputation.

4Shift Budget Toward Higher-Retention Channels

Every dollar spent acquiring a subscriber who stays 1,200+ days (OptIn, Viking, Leadpulse, Retarget) delivers ~12x more lifetime value than a dollar spent on a Fluent subscriber who stays 106 days. Your April budget of $5–7.5K should prioritize Meta lead-form campaigns (current CPL of $3.59 with ~362 day retention on Meta Ads) and Google Demand Gen over Fluent co-reg. Even at a higher CPL, retention matters more than volume for hitting 550K opens.

5Implement Engagement-Based Suppression Before Purges

The recurring bi-weekly purges of ~10K subscribers are necessary but reactive. Consider implementing an automated re-engagement campaign that triggers at 30/60/90 days of non-engagement — send a "We miss you" or "Still interested?" email before removing them. This can recover 5–10% of at-risk subscribers and reduce the purge volumes over time.

6Track CPAS (Cost Per Active Subscriber) Not Just CPL

Adopt Axios's CPEA model: measure the cost per subscriber who opens at least 3 of their first 10 sends. If Fluent CPL is $2 but only 48% stay past 90 days, and of those only 60% are active openers, the real CPAS is closer to $7. Compare this across all acquisition channels to make smarter budget allocation decisions.

The Silver Lining

Organic daily churn has dropped 56% from September to March (698/day → 305/day). The purges are working — the list is getting healthier. The remaining 710K subscribers are increasingly the ones who actually want to be there. The path to 550K opens is about protecting what you have (Gmail fix, welcome sequence, re-engagement) while adding quality subscribers (Meta Ads, referral program) rather than continuing to pour volume through a high-churn channel.
