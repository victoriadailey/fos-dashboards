What GA4 Total Session Views tells us

Parse.ly counts the page a reader lands on when they click a social link — that's 66,183 views. GA4 goes further: it tracks everything that happens after that click, including additional articles browsed and homepage visits within the same session. That's how we get to 76,481 — an additional 10,298 pageviews (16% more) that were invisible before. The gap is smaller than newsletter's 42% uplift, which makes sense: social visitors tend to read one article and leave, while newsletter readers browse deeper. Still, 1 in 6 social visitors explored a second page — valuable secondary reach that Parse.ly alone doesn't capture.

Implement separate UTM parameters for @FOS vs. @FOS_News_ on X
All Channels — UTM Tracking
Do This First

Right now, both @FOS and @FOS_News_ use the same Twitter UTM source tag in article links. That means Parse.ly lumps all X traffic together — we can see that X drove 62,472 page views, but we can't tell how much came from @FOS vs. @FOS_News_. Given that these accounts have very different strategies (headline + image card vs. direct link sharing) and very different post volumes (835 vs. 177), this is critical visibility to unlock.

Action items: (1) Assign distinct UTM campaign or source values — e.g., utm_source=twitter_fos for @FOS and utm_source=twitter_fosnews for @FOS_News_. (2) Update link templates in Sprout (or wherever links are generated) for each account. (3) Coordinate with whoever manages the Parse.ly UTM filters to ensure the new values create separate segments. (4) Once live, this unlocks account-level optimization: we'll finally know which account is the real traffic driver, where to invest more, and how the two accounts work together (or don't).

Cross-functional note: This same UTM hygiene improvement should extend to LinkedIn, Instagram, and any other channel where FOS controls the links. Clean attribution makes every future traffic analysis more actionable — and strengthens any data story.

Consider tracking traffic from FOS reporter social posts
All Channels — Attribution Visibility
Explore

March's non-FOS social referral data (35,704 views from Parse.ly) includes organic shares, Reddit threads, Bluesky posts, and — likely — posts from FOS reporters sharing their own stories from personal accounts. We currently have no way to separate reporter-driven traffic from organic social referrals.

Why this matters: If reporters are driving meaningful traffic from their personal accounts, that's valuable signal for understanding which bylines have social pull, which stories get amplified by their authors, and how much of our "organic" social traffic is actually reporter-driven. This would let us segment reporter traffic in Parse.ly without mixing it into the FOS account data. Worth discussing whether the lift is worth it vs. the coordination required.

Investigate IG Stories link tracking — traffic likely underreported
Instagram — UTM Verification
Quick Fix

Only 9 articles registered Story link sticker clicks in all of March, totaling just 27 views. Given the volume of Stories we post with links, this number doesn’t add up. The most likely explanation: UTM parameters aren’t being applied consistently (or at all) to Story link stickers, meaning that traffic is either going untracked or being attributed to generic “instagram.com” referrals outside of our social segments.

Action items: (1) Audit the current Story link workflow — check whether link stickers are getting FOS-standard UTM parameters when posted via Sprout or manually. (2) If UTMs aren’t being applied, update the posting workflow so every Story link sticker includes utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=stories (or equivalent). (3) After fixing, monitor April Story traffic to establish a real baseline. (4) Compare April Story clicks to March’s 27 to quantify how much traffic was previously invisible.

Expand article post count on LinkedIn
LinkedIn — Scale Quality Traffic
Highest Impact

LinkedIn visitors average 0:49 engaged time — the highest of any channel and nearly 2x the X average. But only 19 articles received LinkedIn traffic this month vs. 375 from X. The quality is proven; the volume opportunity is wide open.

Data Sources & Methodology

Parse.ly Site traffic (source of truth) — Page views, visitors, and engaged minutes to frontofficesports.com. Parse.ly data is segmented by UTM parameters specific to FOS's own social posts — this tracks traffic driven by FOS's posts only, not all social referral traffic to the site. Average engaged time is a pageview-weighted average as reported by Parse.ly — each article's time is weighted by its page views, so high-traffic articles have a larger impact on the average than low-traffic ones.
Parse.ly X / Twitter data note — Parse.ly contains two Twitter UTM segments ("TWITTER" and "Twitter") that reflect a mid-month formatting change in UTM tagging, not a split between @FOS and @FOS_News_. Cross-referencing article titles against Sprout post text confirmed that articles in both segments were posted by both accounts. All X traffic is therefore reported as a single combined channel. Implementing distinct UTMs per account is recommended to enable account-level analysis in future months.
Sprout Post engagement & content audit — Impressions, engagement rate, and content type for posts from @FOS (835 posts), @FOS_News_ (177 posts), FOS on LinkedIn (111 posts), and @frontofficesports on Threads (82 posts). Note: on X, most @FOS posts use article image cards (URL pasted → X generates card preview, strips URL from text). Sprout exports these as "Text" with no visible URL, but the card link is present and clickable. Similar behavior observed on LinkedIn.
GA4 Session-level data — Session-level data filtered to social referral traffic for March 2026. GA4 captures all pageviews within a session that began from a social click. This reveals browsing depth and session quality beyond the Parse.ly landing-page view. Avg session duration, session distribution, and pages per visit derive from GA4 social traffic segments.
Scope: Traffic to frontofficesports.com pages only, driven by FOS's own social posts. Non-FOS destinations excluded.
