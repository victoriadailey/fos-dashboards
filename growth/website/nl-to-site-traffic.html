What GA4 Total Session Views tells us

Parse.ly counts the page a reader lands on when they click a newsletter link — that's 106,689 views. GA4 goes further: it tracks everything that happens after that click, including additional articles browsed, homepage visits, and game pages within the same session. That's how we get to 151,408 — an additional 44,719 pageviews (42% more) that were invisible before. This metric reveals the true value of a newsletter click: readers don't just view one page, they stay and explore.

Newsletter-to-Site Pageviews: 14-Month Trend (Feb 2025 – Mar 2026)

Key Findings

QOTD Restored — Biggest Win This Month

QOTD traffic surged from 338 GA4 views in Feb to 9,518 in March. Visitors who land on QOTD average 2.2 sessions/user and stay 67-81 seconds — it's a session starter, not a dead end. The Mar 24 page optimization pushed retention to its highest level in 14 months.

Factle Is Thriving (Parse.ly Broke)

Parse.ly showed a 77% Factle drop, but GA4 reveals 7,487 Factle views — up 27% from Feb's 5,874. Factle users average 3.1-5.2 sessions/user with 3+ minute sessions. Parse.ly tracking broke mid-March; tracking is back up and running for April.

Returning Visitors Now the Majority

Returning visitors flipped from 41.9% in Feb to 52.8% in March, generating 60,861 views (57% of total). The newsletter is building a habitual readership base — more than half of newsletter-driven users are coming back.

March Broke the Volume + Engagement Trade-Off

In 2025, high-traffic months (Mar-Apr) had shorter sessions (2.1 min), while low-traffic months (Sep) had longer ones (3.6 min). March 2026 is the first month to achieve both high volume (151K views) and deep engagement (3.3 min) — the ideal combination.

Session Duration Surged 43%

Average session duration jumped from 2.3 min in February to 3.3 min in March. Sessions/user has climbed from 1.16 (Mar 2025) to 1.37 (Mar 2026) — an 18% improvement in browsing depth over 12 months.

Parse.ly vs GA4: The Gap Tells a Story

In 2025, GA4 and Parse.ly tracked within 0-10% of each other. In Feb-Mar 2026, GA4 pulled ahead (+6% Feb, +42% Mar) as QOTD restoration and Factle growth drove secondary pageviews that GA4 captures but Parse.ly can't.

February Recommendations — March Status Check

1. Restore QOTD ✅ IMPLEMENTED

QOTD was restored in March. GA4 shows 9,518 views (up from 338 in Feb). Visitors average 2.2 sessions/user — QOTD is a session starter that drives further browsing.

2. UTM Standardization for Tuned In & Asset Class ⚠️ NOT STARTED

Tuned In and Asset Class still lack unique utm_source values. Without these, their site traffic is invisible in Parse.ly. This was a Day 1 recommendation — every month without UTMs is a month of blind optimization.

3. Persistent Factle CTA ✅ ON TRACK

GA4 confirms Factle is thriving: 7,487 views in March, up 27% from Feb. Parse.ly tracking broke mid-month, creating a false alarm. Factle users average 3.1-5.2 sessions/user — the most engaged newsletter-to-site visitors.

4. Continue New Format Rollout ✅ ON TRACK

Month 2 of the Memo Makeover: GA4 session views grew from 110,404 to 151,408 (+37%). Session duration jumped from 2.3 to 3.3 min. The format lift is durable and accelerating.

5. Author Page Optimization ⏳ IN PROGRESS

Author pages got 4,749 GA4 views from newsletter traffic (3.1%), but sessions average only 0.5-1.1 min — well below the 3.3 min site average. Revamped pages with article archives would convert these clicks into deeper engagement.

6. Standardize GA4 Reporting 🆕 RECOMMENDED

This month's GA4 export revealed session-level data that Parse.ly can't capture: 44,700 secondary pageviews, 3.3 min sessions, 1.37 pages per user. Recommend adding GA4 newsletter traffic to the monthly reporting cadence alongside Parse.ly.

Data Sources

GA4 (Google Analytics 4)

Session-level data filtered to newsletter traffic (campaign source = FOS Daily Newsletter). GA4 captures ALL pageviews within a session that began from a newsletter click, revealing secondary browsing, session duration, and pages per session. Exports available for February 2025 through March 2026 with consistent "Average session duration" metric.

Parse.ly

Campaign source "FOS Daily Newsletter" — tracks confirmed landing pageviews on frontofficesports.com. Parse.ly is the established source of truth for the 200K monthly goal. It also provides article-level metadata (section, author, publish date) and engagement time per page.

Campaign Monitor is NOT used for traffic measurement

CM click data includes 83-88% phantom clicks from automated systems and cannot be used to measure actual site visits. CM data may be referenced for edition-level comparisons only.

Key Definitions

GA4 Total Views

All pageviews in sessions where the user arrived via a newsletter click. Includes the landing page plus any other pages browsed during that session.

Parse.ly Landing Views

Pageviews directly attributed to the FOS Daily Newsletter campaign source — the page the reader landed on.

Secondary Pageviews

The difference between GA4 total views and Parse.ly landing views, representing pages browsed after the initial click-through.

Session Duration

GA4's average elapsed time from session start to last recorded event.

Sessions/User

Average number of sessions per active user, indicating browsing depth.

Returning Visitors

Users Parse.ly has seen before from any source — a loyalty indicator.
