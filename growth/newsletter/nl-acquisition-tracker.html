Key Historical Patterns

When Growth Happened

The only sustained growth periods were Nov 2023 – Feb 2024 (when paid spend doubled to $14K/mo, driving 83K net new subs in 4 months) and Jan – Oct 2025 (slow but steady +47K recovery over 10 months). Both periods suggest The Memo can grow when there's consistent investment in acquisition.

When Decline Happened

The most aggressive declines correlate with summer months (Jul-Aug in both 2023 and 2024), list purges (Sep 2023: −17.8K in one month), and Q1 2026 (−88K in 13 weeks — the worst quarter on record). The 2024 decline from peak (787K → 709K) happened over 10 months despite no change in editorial cadence, suggesting organic churn was outpacing acquisition.

The Math: What Does April Need to Look Like?

At 663,358 active subs and a recent open rate of 76-81%, you're currently delivering ~527-537K opens per edition — below the 550K guarantee.

To get back to 550K opens, you need either: (a) ~680K active subscribers at the current ~81% open rate, meaning a net gain of ~17K subs, or (b) a higher open rate on the current base, which means better content targeting and deliverability, or (c) a combination of both — which is the right answer.

At the April plan's projected 1,250-2,250 new leads from paid + referral channels, the key question is what does the churn side look like? If the current ~6,700/week churn rate continues, that's ~27K outgoing in April alone. The acquisition plan covers the new subscriber pipeline; the Campaign Monitor data will tell us if we can slow the outflow enough to close the gap.

Registration Gate: The Leaky Bucket

54% of Registration Gate Signups Unsubscribe

Of 64,272 total registrants who were auto-subscribed to The Memo via the website registration gate, 34,783 (54.1%) have since unsubscribed. Only 29,489 (45.9%) remain. This is a significant forced-subscription problem — people who signed up for site access, not for a newsletter.

When They Leave

The unsub timing tells the story: 6.5% leave same day, 15.4% within the first week, and the largest cohort (35.2%) unsubscribes within 31-90 days. The median time to unsub is 67 days — meaning most forced registrants stick around for about 2 months before leaving. This creates a constant churn headwind.

Retention Recommendations

Action Clean Legacy Paid Acquisition Bounces from List

Fluent and OptIn alone account for 145K bounced emails. If any of these addresses are still on the active list (soft bounces that haven't been fully purged), they're hurting deliverability for everyone. Run a full bounce suppression pass focused on legacy paid sources. This is the single highest-impact deliverability action available.

Rethink Registration Gate Auto-Subscribe Strategy

The gate is creating subscribers who never asked for the newsletter — and 54% of them leave. Consider: (a) making newsletter opt-in a checkbox at registration rather than an automatic subscribe, (b) adding a clear "You're now getting The Memo" welcome email that sets expectations and lets people opt out cleanly on Day 1 rather than unsubscribing weeks later, or (c) segmenting gate registrants into a lighter-touch onboarding sequence before adding to the main list.

Invest Welcome Sequence for All New Subscribers

The 31-90 day unsub window for registration gate users suggests they're getting the newsletter, finding it irrelevant, and leaving. A strong 7-day welcome sequence that showcases The Memo's best content, explains the value prop, and creates an early open habit could meaningfully reduce churn in that critical first-90-days window. This applies to all acquisition channels, not just the gate.
