UTM Tracking — Fix the Blind Spot Before Scaling
Implement separate UTM parameters for @FOS vs. @FOS_News_ on X
Right now, both @FOS and @FOS_News_ use the same Twitter UTM source tag in article links. That means Parse.ly lumps all X traffic together — we can see that X drove 62,472 page views, but we can't tell how much came from @FOS vs. @FOS_News_. Given that these accounts have very different strategies (headline + image card vs. direct link sharing) and very different post volumes (835 vs. 177), this is critical visibility to unlock.

Action items: (1) Assign distinct UTM campaign or source values — e.g., utm_source=twitter_fos for @FOS and utm_source=twitter_fosnews for @FOS_News_. (2) Update link templates in Sprout (or wherever links are generated) for each account. (3) Coordinate with whoever manages the Parse.ly UTM filters to ensure the new values create separate segments. (4) Once live, this unlocks account-level optimization: we'll finally know which account is the real traffic driver, where to invest more, and how the two accounts work together (or don't).

Cross-functional note: This same UTM hygiene improvement should extend to LinkedIn, Instagram, and any other channel where FOS controls the links. Clean attribution makes every future traffic analysis more actionable — and strengthens any data story for sales and partnerships.

LinkedIn visitors average 0:49 engaged time — the highest of any channel and nearly 2x the X average. But only 19 articles received LinkedIn traffic this month vs. 375 from X. The quality is proven; the volume opportunity is wide open.

Action items: (1) Post FOS article links more frequently — aim for 4–5 link posts per week alongside the existing video/carousel/photo content. (2) Ensure every FOS exclusive and feature gets a LinkedIn link post with UTM parameters. (3) For carousel and photo posts that cover an FOS article's topic, add the article link in the first comment. (4) Prioritize features and long-form pieces — the Mike Repole story generated 1.6 min avg engaged time, and the Trump roundtable piece drew 253 page views with deep engagement. This is exactly the kind of content LinkedIn's audience wants to read in full.

Cross-functional note: LinkedIn drives the highest-quality visitors by engaged time — exactly the profile that matters for B2B advertising and sponsorship sales. Growing LinkedIn traffic directly strengthens the value prop for sponsored content partnerships.

@frontofficesports on Threads — The Biggest Untested Opportunity
Run a structured traffic test — Threads has never been optimized for site traffic
We can't call Threads a failed traffic channel when we've only linked to 1 article in an entire month. The platform generated 1.1M impressions and 7,419 engagements from 82 posts — the audience is active and engaged. Threads' text-heavy format is structurally similar to X, where the same type of content (headlines + article links) is currently driving 62,472 page views. The difference isn't the platform — it's that we haven't tried.

Action items: (1) Run a 4-week test: include FOS article links in at least 3–5 Threads posts per week, mirroring the headline + link format that works on X. (2) Use UTM parameters on every link (utm_source=threads&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=fos) for clean attribution. (3) Test different formats — headline-first posts with the link at the end, quote-style posts that tease the article, and direct "read more" CTAs. (4) Benchmark against X's conversion rate: if Threads can drive even 1–2% of X's volume with dedicated effort, that's 600–1,200+ incremental monthly page views from a channel that currently contributes 8.

Why this matters now: Threads is still in its growth phase, and early movers who figure out the traffic playbook will have a structural advantage. FOS already has the audience (1.1M impressions/month) — this is about converting attention that already exists into site traffic.

Data Sources & Methodology

Parse.ly Site traffic (source of truth) — Page views, visitors, and engaged minutes to frontofficesports.com. Parse.ly data is segmented by UTM parameters specific to FOS's own social posts — this tracks traffic driven by FOS's posts only, not all social referral traffic to the site. Average engaged time is a pageview-weighted average as reported by Parse.ly — each article's time is weighted by its page views, so high-traffic articles have a larger impact on the average than low-traffic ones.
Parse.ly X / Twitter data note — Parse.ly contains two Twitter UTM segments ("TWITTER" and "Twitter") that reflect a mid-month formatting change in UTM tagging, not a split between @FOS and @FOS_News_. Cross-referencing article titles against Sprout post text confirmed that articles in both segments were posted by both accounts. All X traffic is therefore reported as a single combined channel. Implementing distinct UTMs per account is recommended to enable account-level analysis in future months.
Sprout Social Post engagement & content audit — Impressions, engagement rate, and content type for posts from @FOS (835 posts), @FOS_News_ (177 posts), FOS on LinkedIn (111 posts), and @frontofficesports on Threads (82 posts). Note: on X, most @FOS posts use article image cards (URL pasted → X generates card preview, strips URL from text). Sprout exports these as "Text" with no visible URL, but the card link is present and clickable. Similar behavior observed on LinkedIn.
Scope: Traffic to frontofficesports.com pages only, driven by FOS's own social posts. Non-FOS destinations excluded.
Threads sample size note: Threads traffic data reflects only 1 article linked across 82 posts in March. This is insufficient to evaluate the platform's traffic potential. March data serves as a baseline; a dedicated traffic test is recommended before drawing conclusions.
